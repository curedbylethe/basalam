`DrawBorder` paints a one-cell ring around a rectangle of a [`Buffer`](/reference/buffer). It is the shared helper that every frame in basalam uses, including the [`Panel`](/reference/panel) widget, so a custom widget that wants a frame calls it too. It draws only the ring. It does not lay out children, change geometry, or pick a color tier.

```salam
basalam.DrawBorder(buf, bounds, basalam.BorderStyle.Single, basalam.Style { fg = basalam.Color.Ansi(4) }, "Files")
```

The signature is `DrawBorder(buf: Buffer, rect: Rect, border: BorderStyle, style: Style, title: str)`. The `border` argument is the `BorderStyle` value itself, not a wrapper.

## Border styles

`BorderStyle` has four values. `None` draws nothing; the other three draw a ring with these glyphs:

| Style | Top left | Top | Top right | Sides | Bottom left | Bottom | Bottom right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Single` | `┌` | `─` | `┐` | `│` | `└` | `─` | `┘` |
| `Double` | `╔` | `═` | `╗` | `║` | `╚` | `═` | `╝` |
| `Rounded` | `╭` | `─` | `╮` | `│` | `╰` | `─` | `╯` |

`Rounded` uses the single-line edges with arc corners. `None` writes no cell and no byte, and it does not reserve any space. A ring is one cell wide, so drawing one inside a rectangle leaves the inner area for content: the content area is the rectangle inset by one cell on each side.

## Title

The `title` sits on the top edge, starting two cells in from the left corner, with one space before and after it:

```text
┌─ Files ──────┐
```

- The title is trimmed of surrounding whitespace. An empty or whitespace-only title draws nothing.
- The title needs a width of at least 6 cells and a height of at least 2. A narrower rectangle draws no title.
- The available span is the width minus 5 cells. A longer title is cut to that span on the right. There is no ellipsis and no centering.
- A title never reaches a corner, because the corners are drawn in every case and the title stops before the right edge.

The title uses the same foreground as the ring. There is no separate title style.

## Degenerate rectangles

Small rectangles follow fixed rules, not a clip:

| Rectangle | Draws |
| --- | --- |
| `width` or `height` is zero or negative | Nothing |
| `height` 1 | A horizontal rule of `─` across `width`, no corners, no title |
| `width` 1 and `height` 2 or more | A vertical rule of `│`, no corners, no title |
| 2 by 2 | Four corners, no edges |
| 2 by 3 | Corners and two `│` edge cells in the middle row |
| 3 by 2 | Corners and a `─` edge between them on each row |

When the rectangle is larger than the buffer, the ring is clipped at the buffer's edge, and the part that falls inside the buffer is drawn in its correct place. A ring is never shifted to fit.

## Color

Every ring cell, including the title, uses one foreground color, `style.fg`. The rules are:

- Only `fg` is used. `bg` and the attributes `bold`, `dim`, `italic`, and `underline` are ignored, even when set.
- A `fg` of `Color.Default` writes no escape, so the frame keeps the terminal's own foreground.
- The frame is lowered at emission like every other styled cell: the buffer applies its `tier`, so an `Rgb` foreground becomes the nearest ANSI colour on a 16-colour terminal and no colour at all at `Off`. `DrawBorder` takes no tier parameter and reads none.

The glyphs are the same at every color tier, so a frame keeps its shape wherever it runs.

## Writes and overlap

`DrawBorder` replaces every cell it draws and reads or clears nothing else. The interior of the rectangle keeps whatever was drawn there before, so you can draw a frame over content. A `Screen` resolves junctions where a ring cell has a neighbour pointing into it; a hand-built `Buffer` does not, because it has no paint phase.

## Glyph sets

A glyph set is a `BorderGlyphs` value. Six are built in, each a function that returns the set:

| Set | Run (`h`, `v`) | Corners (tl, tr, bl, br) | T down, up, right, left | Cross | `hw`, `vw` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `GlyphsSingle()` | `─` `│` | `┌ ┐ └ ┘` | `┬ ┴ ├ ┤` | `┼` | `Light`, `Light` |
| `GlyphsDouble()` | `═` `║` | `╔ ╗ ╚ ╝` | `╦ ╩ ╠ ╣` | `╬` | `Double`, `Double` |
| `GlyphsRounded()` | `─` `│` | `╭ ╮ ╰ ╯` | `┬ ┴ ├ ┤` | `┼` | `Light`, `Light` |
| `GlyphsASCII()` | `-` `\|` | `+ + + +` | `+ + + +` | `+` | `Light`, `Light` |
| `GlyphsHeavy()` | `━` `┃` | `┏ ┓ ┗ ┛` | `┳ ┻ ┣ ┫` | `╋` | `Heavy`, `Heavy` |
| `GlyphsMixed()` | `━` `│` | `┍ ┑ ┕ ┙` | `┯ ┷ ┝ ┥` | `┿` | `Heavy`, `Light` |

```salam
pub enum Weight:
    Light
    Heavy
    Double
end

pub struct BorderGlyphs:
    pub h: str = ""
    pub v: str = ""
    pub tl: str = ""
    pub tr: str = ""
    pub bl: str = ""
    pub br: str = ""
    pub t_down: str = ""
    pub t_up: str = ""
    pub t_right: str = ""
    pub t_left: str = ""
    pub cross: str = ""
    pub hw: Weight = Weight.Light
    pub vw: Weight = Weight.Light
end

pub func GlyphsFor(border: BorderStyle, policy: GlyphPolicy): BorderGlyphs
pub func DrawBorderGlyphs(buf: Buffer, rect: Rect, glyphs: BorderGlyphs, style: Style, title: str)
```

`h` draws the top and bottom edges and `v` the left and right edges. Every glyph is one codepoint. The `t_*` and `cross` fields, and `hw` and `vw`, are data for callers: `DrawBorderGlyphs` draws only the edges and corners. Junction glyphs come from the `Screen` pass, which reads the arms each cell shows rather than these fields.

`GlyphsFor` maps a `BorderStyle` to a set. `None` gives the zero value with no glyphs. Under `GlyphPolicy.ASCII` every other style gives `GlyphsASCII()`. Otherwise `Single`, `Double`, and `Rounded` give the set of the same name. `DrawBorder(buf, rect, border, style, title)` is `DrawBorderGlyphs(buf, rect, GlyphsFor(border, buf.glyphs), style, title)`, so every rule on this page applies to both.

`BorderStyle` keeps its four values. `GlyphsHeavy()`, `GlyphsMixed()`, and custom sets are used through `DrawBorderGlyphs`, which does not change the content inset: a ring is still one cell, and `BorderInset` is `1` for every style except `None`.

```salam
basalam.DrawBorderGlyphs(buf, bounds, basalam.GlyphsHeavy(), basalam.Style { fg = basalam.Color.Ansi(1) }, "Alert")
```

### Glyph policy precedence

The ring follows the [`glyphs` policy](/reference/buffer#glyph-policy) of the buffer it draws into. Under `GlyphPolicy.ASCII`, `DrawBorderGlyphs` replaces the glyph set it receives with `GlyphsASCII()`, including a custom set, so the title cells, the corners, and the `height` 1 and `width` 1 rules all stay ASCII. The default policy is `Unicode`. `Screen.start()` detects the policy from the environment once unless you call [`set_glyphs`](/reference/screen#terminal-lifecycle) first. No widget reads the environment.

## Nested rings

A [`Panel`](/reference/panel) insets its child by one cell, so a `Panel` inside a `Panel` draws two rings with no gap between them. Each ring keeps its own glyph set: an outer `Single` ring and an inner `Double` ring show `┌` at the outer corner and `╔` one cell diagonally inside it.

## Junctions

After widgets paint, `Screen` rewrites a box-drawing cell when an orthogonal neighbour points into it. The added arm points toward the adjacent cell: a neighbour above adds `UP`, and a neighbour below adds `DOWN`; neighbours to the left or right add `LEFT` or `RIGHT`.

Light, Heavy, Double, and the four mixed-weight families join. If a combination has no Unicode glyph, such as Heavy against Double, the current glyph stays unchanged. The pass never removes or reweights an existing arm. A replacement keeps the colour of the cell it replaces.

`GlyphPolicy.ASCII` disables the pass, so ASCII frames stay unchanged. Title spaces occupy top-edge cells and have no arms, so no junction forms there. The pass rewrites mapped box-drawing characters, regardless of whether a built-in or custom glyph set supplied them. Unmapped characters stay unchanged.

This is `Screen` behaviour. `DrawBorder` into a hand-built `Buffer` draws the ring only. The two rings of a `Panel` inside a `Panel` stay parallel and do not join: adjacent cells point along the same axis, so they are not a junction.

## Out of scope

These are not part of this release:

- Shadows. They need the same paint-phase contract.

## Use it in a widget

Call `DrawBorder` in your widget's `render`, with the bounds the screen gives you:

```salam
pub func render(buf: basalam.Buffer, bounds: basalam.Rect):
    basalam.DrawBorder(buf, bounds, basalam.BorderStyle.Rounded, basalam.Style { fg = basalam.Color.Ansi(6) }, "Log")
end
```

To frame a child widget without writing a frame yourself, use [`Panel`](/reference/panel).
