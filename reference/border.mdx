`DrawBorder` paints a one-cell ring around a rectangle of a [`Buffer`](/reference/buffer). It is the shared helper that every frame in basalam uses, including the [`Panel`](/reference/panel) widget, so a custom widget that wants a frame calls it too. It draws only the ring. It does not lay out children, change geometry, or pick a color tier.

```salam
basalam.DrawBorder(buf, bounds, basalam.BorderStyle.Single, basalam.Style { fg = basalam.Color.Ansi(4) }, "Files")
```

The signature is `DrawBorder(buf: Buffer, rect: Rect, border: BorderStyle, style: Style, title: str)`. The `border` argument is the `BorderStyle` value itself, not a wrapper.

## Border styles

`BorderStyle` has four values. `None` draws nothing; the other three draw a ring with these glyphs:

| Style | Top left | Top | Top right | Sides | Bottom left | Bottom | Bottom right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Single` | `┌` | `─` | `┐` | `│` | `└` | `─` | `┘` |
| `Double` | `╔` | `═` | `╗` | `║` | `╚` | `═` | `╝` |
| `Rounded` | `╭` | `─` | `╮` | `│` | `╰` | `─` | `╯` |

`Rounded` uses the single-line edges with arc corners. `None` writes no cell and no byte, and it does not reserve any space. A ring is one cell wide, so drawing one inside a rectangle leaves the inner area for content: the content area is the rectangle inset by one cell on each side.

## Title

The `title` sits on the top edge, starting two cells in from the left corner, with one space before and after it:

```text
┌─ Files ──────┐
```

- The title is trimmed of surrounding whitespace. An empty or whitespace-only title draws nothing.
- The title needs a width of at least 6 cells and a height of at least 2. A narrower rectangle draws no title.
- The available span is the width minus 5 cells. A longer title is cut to that span on the right. There is no ellipsis and no centering.
- A title never reaches a corner, because the corners are drawn in every case and the title stops before the right edge.

The title uses the same foreground as the ring. There is no separate title style.

## Degenerate rectangles

Small rectangles follow fixed rules, not a clip:

| Rectangle | Draws |
| --- | --- |
| `width` or `height` is zero or negative | Nothing |
| `height` 1 | A horizontal rule of `─` across `width`, no corners, no title |
| `width` 1 and `height` 2 or more | A vertical rule of `│`, no corners, no title |
| 2 by 2 | Four corners, no edges |
| 2 by 3 | Corners and two `│` edge cells in the middle row |
| 3 by 2 | Corners and a `─` edge between them on each row |

When the rectangle is larger than the buffer, the ring is clipped at the buffer's edge, and the part that falls inside the buffer is drawn in its correct place. A ring is never shifted to fit.

## Color

Every ring cell, including the title, uses one foreground color, `style.fg`. The rules are:

- Only `fg` is used. `bg` and the attributes `bold`, `dim`, `italic`, and `underline` are ignored, even when set.
- A `fg` of `Color.Default` writes no escape, so the frame keeps the terminal's own foreground.
- Border cells carry the foreground at true color, as `Text` does. The frame is not lowered to the terminal's color tier when it is drawn.

The glyphs are the same at every color tier, so a frame keeps its shape wherever it runs.

## Writes and overlap

`DrawBorder` replaces every cell it draws and reads or clears nothing else. The interior of the rectangle keeps whatever was drawn there before, so you can draw a frame over content. Overlapping frames are the caller's concern: the last write wins, and junctions are not resolved.

## Out of scope

These are not part of this release:

- ASCII fallback glyphs for terminals without box drawing.
- Heavy or mixed-weight glyph sets.
- Junctions where two rings meet, and nested frames.
- Shadows.

## Use it in a widget

Call `DrawBorder` in your widget's `render`, with the bounds the screen gives you:

```salam
pub func render(buf: basalam.Buffer, bounds: basalam.Rect):
    basalam.DrawBorder(buf, bounds, basalam.BorderStyle.Rounded, basalam.Style { fg = basalam.Color.Ansi(6) }, "Log")
end
```

To frame a child widget without writing a frame yourself, use [`Panel`](/reference/panel).
